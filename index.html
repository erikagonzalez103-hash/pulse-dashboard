Question 01 · For Wednesday

Vendor approval — who gets in, and how do we decide?

A brand, strategy, and operations decision. Is Pulse open (anyone can sign up), curated (apply, get reviewed), or invitation-only (we reach out)? The magician stress-test surfaced the gap.

Working direction

Two-door model. Concierge / invitation path gets a short confirmation form (Pulse already qualified them in conversation). Self-serve path gets the full application with operational fitness questions: story, what they sell, capacity, what a busy night looks like, overflow handling.

Launch posture: invitation-only, evolving to curated application as Pulse scales.

✓ Decided May 27 · Erika: Revenue question removed from the self-serve application. Same instinct already led the team to remove it from the HER House Activation form.

  • ? Who reviews self-serve applications? SLA — 48 hours? A week?
  • ? Decline language — nobody is “not good enough,” they might be “not the right fit for this phase.”
Question 07

Content moderation policy — what we screen for, and how we reject

The principle is decided: Pulse moderates floor (foul language, unsupported claims, illegal content, policy violations), never ceiling. The vendor's voice is their own. But the operational specifics — what exactly we screen for, what the rejection language says, and who reviews appeals — still need a real policy document before we ship.

Working list of what we screen for

Profanity & slurs (clear-cut, automatable) · discriminatory language (edge cases need human review) · unsupported superlatives like “best in Dallas” (authenticity guardrail) · engine-violating mechanics (discount language in an exclusive offer) · illegal content · misleading info (hidden fees, fake urgency).

For team decision (future meeting)
  • ? Who writes the actual policy document? By when?
  • ? Who reviews appeals at standards@pulse.app? What's the SLA?
  • ? Publish the policy to vendors at sign-up, or surface relevant parts at rejection?

Recent decisions logged
✓ May 27 Voice splits at registration

Before registration: persuasive register OK (confident-and-selective, not salesy). After registration: elegant, authoritative without being cold, concise, says just enough to explain. Tell vendors what each thing is, never sell them on why.

✓ May 27 Vendor voice is their own

Pulse provides structure, not voice. AI Polish tightens the vendor's own words; never translates to a house voice. Pulse moderates floor (foul language, unsupported claims, illegal content), never ceiling.

✓ May 27 Engine determines mechanics

Standard offers allow discount language (used carefully). Exclusive offers do not — in luxury, ubiquity kills the brand. Moderation is engine-aware: discount language in an exclusive offer is a violation of the vendor's own earlier choice.

✓ May 27 Revenue question removed

Annual revenue is cut from the self-serve vendor application. Most established vendors won't disclose it on a sign-up form, and the same instinct already led the team to remove it from the HER House Activation form.

✓ May 27 Vendor approval — two-door model (working direction)

Concierge path = short confirmation. Self-serve path = full application with operational fitness questions. Launch invitation-only, evolving to curated application as Pulse scales. Full team confirmation pending Wednesday.

✓ May 26 Build sequencing — design here, wire in Claude Code

Design + clickable prototype in chat. Real Supabase auth, Storage, and analytics queries wired in Claude Code. The prototype becomes the unambiguous spec; the wiring is faster and cleaner.

✓ May 26 Image pipeline — automatic brand treatment

Vendor uploads raw photo → Pulse auto-applies gold-tint, vignette, crop. No manual photo editor. Stored in Supabase Storage; treatment renders via CSS so raw image stays once.

✓ May 26 Concierge / SPOC relationship

Brand

Visual Language

Elegance with edge — a Fraunces wordmark leading, Cinzel carrying carved-stone authority in the subheads, near-black depth as the canvas, gold as the accent that signals heat, and a deep cool red as the brand's pulse. Sharper edges than typical app design; brighter lights than typical luxury.

Primary Mark
The compass-heart: a dimensional gold compass with a heartbeat traced through a red heart. Navigation, aliveness, and the palette in one mark. Use at all sizes — app icon, social, standalone identity.
In motion
The mark's heartbeat is its animation source — it pulses on loading states and "live now" surfaces. The signature check-in lub-dub haptic + gold bloom mirror its rhythm.
See the Screens section to feel it animate.

The full Pulse logo system — every lockup, the icon and small-size variants, the reverse and monochrome treatments, and the core palette in one place. The wordmark is set in Fraunces; the compass-heart mark carries the signature.

Using these. This is the current suite preview — grab any lockup you need with a snip / screenshot tool for slides, docs, or quick mockups. For the Team

Using This Hub

This page is the project's front door. The actual editing of documents happens elsewhere — and that's by design. The hub stays clean and orienting; collaboration happens where collaboration tools are good.

01
Read here, edit there
Use this hub to orient. For live editing and comments, put the docx files in Google Drive or Notion. Update the download links in this page to point to the live versions.
02
Deploy in five minutes
Drop this folder onto Netlify Drop or push to a GitHub Pages repo. Both are free and produce a shareable URL the team can bookmark. Files in the same folder become downloadable links automatically.
03
Update as it grows
Add new artifacts as cards in the relevant section (Research, Strategy, Investors). Keep the metadata strip honest — page count and last-updated date matter more than they look like they do.
04
Suggested team setup
Hub on Netlify (orientation). Docs in Google Drive (editing). Tasks in Linear or a simple Notion board (work). Comms in Slack. The hub is the front door; the rest is the house.
